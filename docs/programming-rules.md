### Week evaluation

#### `evaluation.score_weights`

A week starts at 100. Each avoid finding costs 25 and each prefer finding 8, at half that when the plan explains why it keeps it.

- **Why:** Four unexplained avoid findings leave nothing, because each risks injury or a session done badly; advice costs little; a kept trade-off still tires the body, so it still costs.
- **Source:** `SCORE_WEIGHTS` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/evaluation.ts`

```json

```

#### `evaluation.grades`

A week is clear with nothing ahead to act on, minor when what is ahead is advice or explained, and has conflicts when an unexplained avoid finding is still ahead.

- **Why:** Days already past cannot change, so the grade speaks only of what the owner can still act on.
- **Source:** `GRADES` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/evaluation.ts`

```json
{
  "conflicts": "An avoid rule is broken on a day still ahead, and nothing in the plan explains it.",

}
```

#### `evaluation.resolution_order`

A kept finding gives one reason, the first that applies: your own move, fixed commitments, a goal’s priority, then no free day.

- **Why:** Your own choice says most about why the week looks this way; a commitment cannot move; the rest are the plan’s own trade-offs.
- **Source:** `RESOLUTION_ORDER` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/evaluation.ts`


#### `evaluation.actionable`

A finding is actionable while its later session is today or still ahead; skipped sessions are not judged.

- **Why:** A move or an easier session can still fix it; once its days have passed it is history, not advice.
- **Applied by:** `evaluateWeek` in `src/lib/domain/programming/evaluate/week.ts`

A logged session with no RPE or no duration has an unknown load: it is counted and named, and left out of every total, never taken as 0.

#### `fatigue.half_lives`

Each system’s leftover fatigue halves over its own half-life: legs 2.5 days, upper body, combat and the nervous system 2, aerobic 1.5.

- **Why:** Systems recover at different speeds, so a hard leg day still counts two days later when an easy run is long forgotten.

#### `fatigue.ratio`

Each system’s acute:chronic ratio is the one Progress shows (7- and 28-day averages), from 28 days of history. While a system still carries a hard session’s fatigue, 1.3 makes it at least elevated and 1.5 one level worse.

- **Why:** A fast rise in load is a warning only while that load is still with you: one session a week spikes the ratio every week without any rise.
- **Source:** `RATIO` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/fatigue.ts`

#### `fatigue.residual`

A system is elevated when its leftover fatigue is above 1.2 × its typical hard session, and overloaded above 1.75 ×.

- **Why:** One hard session leaves about 1 ×; more means fatigue is stacking, and a steady week peaks near 1.5 ×, so overload starts above it.
- **Source:** `RESIDUAL` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/fatigue.ts`

```json

  "overloaded": 1.75,
  "why": "One hard session leaves a system at about 1 × its typical hard session; more than 1.2 × means fatigue is stacking. A steady week ends its hardest day at about 1.5 × (the legs, after squats, pads and deadlifts), so overload starts above that, at 1.75 ×."
}
```

#### `fatigue.history`

A system’s typical hard session is the median of its hardest session each week: from the plan until there are 28 days of history, then from the last 28 days of logs.

- **Why:** Fatigue is judged against your own training, so the same day reads the same for a beginner and an athlete.
- **Source:** `HISTORY` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/fatigue.ts`

#### `fatigue.model`

Logged sessions replace planned ones up to today: a completed session counts once, a skipped one never, and one never logged counts 0 and is named missed. Estimated load is its own series, never in a logged total.

- **Why:** The model reads what you actually did and what is still ahead, so Train, the coach and the weekly review agree with Progress.
- **Applied by:** `loadModel` in `src/lib/domain/programming/load/fatigue.ts`

#### `concurrent.residual_fatigue_before_hard`

- **Why:** A system still carrying more than its usual fatigue cannot give a hard session its best, and pushing it again raises the injury risk.

### Hypertrophy blocks

9 rules, from `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/registry/hypertrophy.ts`.

#### `hypertrophy.start`

A hypertrophy block’s first week starts at the low end of the split’s hard-set band per major group (10 of 10–20; 8 on a cut).

- **Why:** Starting at the least volume that still builds muscle leaves the whole range to grow into.
- **Source:** `HARD_SET_RANGES.hypertrophy` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/volume.ts`

```json
{ "sets": [10, 20], "suits": "building muscle" }
```

#### `hypertrophy.step`

Each building week adds 1 set per group (2 for intermediate and advanced lifters, and on a bulk), never more than 20 % over the week before; each week’s range is 2 sets wide.

- **Why:** One or two sets a week, never a fifth more, is a rise recovery keeps up with; a surplus recovers faster, so a bulk climbs at the top step.
- **Source:** `HYPERTROPHY.step` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/hypertrophy.ts`

#### `hypertrophy.ceiling`

No week goes above the top of the band (20 sets per group; 16 on a cut, 80 % of the top).

- **Why:** Past the top of the range more sets add fatigue faster than they add muscle.
- **Source:** `HARD_SET_RANGES.hypertrophy` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/volume.ts`

#### `hypertrophy.deload`

The block ends in a deload at 60 % of the first week’s sets, RPE 6 at most, loads 10 % lighter.

- **Why:** About 40 % fewer sets than the block began with lets the accumulated fatigue clear, so the block’s growth shows.

#### `hypertrophy.reps`

Compounds are done for 6–10 reps, isolations for 10–15, and isolations for small muscles (arms, calves, core) for 12–20.

- **Why:** Heavier compounds work best in lower reps, single-joint work in moderate ones, and small muscles recover fast enough for high reps.

```json
}
```

#### `hypertrophy.rpe`

The top set rises from RPE 7 to 8, 8.5 and 9 over the building weeks, and an isolation’s last set goes half a point higher, to 9.5 in the last week.

- **Why:** Effort rises as the block goes on, so the early weeks leave room to add sets and the last ones take sets close to failure, where isolations are safe to push.

#### `hypertrophy.block`

A hypertrophy block is 4 to 6 building weeks (4 by default), then one deload week.

- **Why:** Four to six weeks of rising volume is as long as recovery keeps pace before a deload is needed.
- **Source:** `HYPERTROPHY.block` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/hypertrophy.ts`

### Endurance blocks

13 rules, from `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/registry/endurance.ts`.

#### `endurance.phases`

A marathon block has 4–6 base and 6–8 build weeks, then 3 peak and 3 taper weeks; a half 3–4 base, 4–6 build, 2 peak and 2 taper. A short runway shortens the base first and keeps the taper.

- **Why:** Base builds the mileage, build adds the race-specific work and peak holds the biggest weeks; when time is short the base gives way first, because the taper and the race-specific weeks matter most on race day.
- **Source:** `ENDURANCE.phases` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/endurance.ts`

#### `endurance.increase`

Each week’s running grows at most 10 % over the biggest week before it (peak weeks hold it).

- **Why:** Mileage that grows by a tenth a week at most gives tendons and bones time to adapt, which is where running injuries start.
- **Source:** `ENDURANCE.increase` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/endurance.ts`

#### `endurance.regain`

The one exception to 10 %: a weekly mileage held in the last 8 weeks may be regained at up to 20 % a week, never above it, and the week says so.

- **Why:** Mileage you ran in the last two months comes back faster than new mileage builds, so regaining it may go up to a fifth a week.
- **Source:** `ENDURANCE.regain` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/endurance.ts`


#### `endurance.cutback`

Every 4th week of base and build is a cutback at 80 % of the biggest week so far.

- **Why:** A lighter week every fourth lets the body absorb three weeks of growth, so the build stays sustainable.
- **Source:** `ENDURANCE.cutback` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/endurance.ts`

#### `paces.vdot`

A race sets a VDOT by the Daniels–Gilbert equations: the running cost of its speed over the share of VO2max a race that long is run at.

- **Why:** Daniels’ Running Formula rests on these equations, so one race predicts every other distance and every training pace, published and checked against his tables.

The equations read races from 1500 m to the marathon; a race outside them is carried to the nearer end by Riegel’s formula first.

Before any stage runs, the goals are put in order of their effective priority on the block’s first day, the leader first, and each is marked primary, secondary or held at maintenance.

A new program holds the season’s blocks: the first planned week by week, then outlines to each event, with every block type, taper, deload and dose recorded. With every goal paused, it is the maintenance plan as before.

#### `strength.substituted`

When the usual exercise for a slot may not be prescribed, its best eligible alternative takes the slot, or else the slot’s best eligible candidate.

- **Why:** An injury, missing equipment or a dislike changes only the slots it rules out, and the slot keeps its training intent.
- **Applied by:** `selectWeek` in `src/lib/domain/programming/selection.ts`

#### `strength.disliked`

An exercise you dislike is never prescribed.

- **Why:** A plan you enjoy is one you follow; there is nearly always another exercise for the same purpose.
- **Applied by:** `selectWeek` in `src/lib/domain/programming/selection.ts`

#### `strength.unmapped_avoidance`

A movement to avoid that the coach could not match to any exercise is recorded, so you can say it another way.

- **Why:** Nothing is ruled out silently, and nothing you asked to avoid is ignored silently either.
- **Applied by:** `selectWeek` in `src/lib/domain/programming/selection.ts`

#### `strength.slot_empty`

When no eligible exercise fits a slot, the slot stays empty with a decision saying why.

- **Why:** An exercise you cannot do safely, or with the kit you have, is worse than none.
- **Applied by:** `selectWeek` in `src/lib/domain/programming/selection.ts`

Every strength discipline trains in the strength style, with your baseline lifts in its main slots, unless it has a style of its own.

#### `strength.working_rpe`

Work other than the top and back-off sets is held to RPE 7, or the week’s cap if that is lower.

- **Why:** Secondary and accessory work supports the main lifts, so it stops a few reps short of failure and leaves recovery for them.
- **Source:** `WORKING_RPE` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/strength.ts`

```json
7
```

#### `strength.intensity_by_rpe`

A strength session’s intensity comes from its week’s RPE cap: under 6.5 easy, under 7.5 moderate, under 9 hard and very hard from 9.

- **Why:** How hard a week may go sets how hard its sessions are, so the interference rules and the load model read each session by it.
- **Source:** `INTENSITY_BY_RPE` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/strength.ts`

```json
]
```

- **Source:** `LOAD_RULES` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/loads.ts`

A measured 1RM loads week 1 at 92.5 % of what the RPE table gives its reps at the week’s cap.

- **Why:** About an RPE under the cap, in case the max has moved since it was tested.

```json
0.925
```

An estimated 1RM loads 2.5 % less of it than a measured one, every week.

- **Why:** An estimate is never treated as a measured max.
- **Source:** `LOAD_RULES.estimateDiscount` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/loads.ts`

#### `loads.baseline_set`

- **Why:** Guessing a load is riskier than finding it: the top set measures where you are.
- **Applied by:** `loadBaseline` in `src/lib/domain/programming/loads.ts`

- **Source:** `FATIGUE_RULES.signals` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/results.ts`

```json
{
  "acwr": ["above", 1.5],
  "monotony": ["above", 2],
  "soreness": ["at_least", 4],
  "sleepHours": ["below", 6.5],
  "rpeCreep": ["at_least", 0.5],
  "liftsMissingReps": ["at_least", 2],
  "decliningLifts": ["at_least", 1]
}
```

#### `results.fatigue_high`

Fatigue is watch with one signal met and high, which recommends a deload, with 2 or more.

- **Why:** One sign may be a bad night or a hard week; several together are a pattern worth a lighter week.
- **Source:** `FATIGUE_RULES.highSignals` in `src/lib/domain/programming/rules/results.ts`

```json
2
```

#### `next_week.deload`

High fatigue on a normal week deloads the main lifts: 40 % fewer sets and RPE at most 6; a deloaded week takes no progression.

- **Why:** High fatigue recommends a deload, and cutting the main lifts’ volume and effort clears it fastest without stopping training.
- **Source:** `DELOAD` in `src/lib/domain/programming/review/next-week.ts`

```json
{ "setsFactor": 0.6, "rpeCap": 6 }
```

#### `next_week.light_weeks`

In a week already light (deload, taper, event, recovery), high fatigue only caps the main lifts' RPE at 6 and flags the cycle review.

- **Why:** A week that is already light needs no second cut; if fatigue stays high through it, the next block should know.
- **Source:** `LIGHT_WEEK_KINDS` in `src/lib/domain/programming/review/next-week.ts`

```json
["deload", "taper", "event", "recovery"]
```

#### `next_week.max_ops`

- **Source:** `MAX_NEXT_WEEK_OPS` in `src/lib/domain/programming/review/next-week.ts`

#### `next_week.priority`
